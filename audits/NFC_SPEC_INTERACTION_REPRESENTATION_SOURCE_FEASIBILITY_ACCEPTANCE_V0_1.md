## RH review

PASS.

The audit correctly identifies a real operator-origin pattern in the RH branch: the scaling-flow transfer operator is branch-locally reconstructed from declared transformed probes plus Mellin-weighted aggregation rather than introduced as a primitive. This is materially relevant to the SPEC problem.

The audit also correctly refuses to promote that result into SPEC. The RH construction depends on RH-specific Mellin/Dirichlet descent, arithmetic scaling/orbit data, SCC admissibility, and a branch-specific weighting architecture. No frozen bridge identifies those objects with SPEC probes or response channels.

The RH half-density and log-derivative weights therefore cannot be reinterpreted as physical transition-strength weights.

## LING review

PASS.

LING supplies a genuine action/composition architecture: declared assembly operations act on quotient-visible objects; context frames induce context-response probes; context extension is closed; and equivalence is a congruence under declared composition.

The audit correctly treats this as a branch-local schema rather than a physical interaction representation. The LING grammar-descent object governs admissible composition in the contrast-visible sector but is not an operator on the SPEC spectral arena and carries no transition-strength authority.

## Combined-schema review

PASS.

The synthesis

`lawful action -> family of transformed actions -> justified aggregation -> operator`

is a useful design schema and accurately reflects the complementary RH/LING lessons.

It is not yet theorem-bearing for SPEC because frozen SPEC lacks a canonical probe action in the operator arena and lacks a justified aggregation/linearization rule.

## Nonuniqueness review

PASS.

The response assignment alone underdetermines representation type. Multiple inequivalent mathematical representations can preserve the same observable response relation. The YM-derived inner product does not eliminate that underdetermination because no canonical embedding of the probe into the operator arena is established.

Thus a unique `J_SPEC` is not source-forced.

## Outcome-E review

PASS.

Outcome E is properly withheld. RH and LING provide important candidate schemas but do not provide a lawful cross-branch SPEC bridge satisfying the preregistered bridge and uniqueness conditions.

## Accepted frontier

The interaction problem is usefully decomposed as:

1. `O-SPEC.ACT`: canonical typed probe action on quotient-visible SPEC objects;
2. `O-SPEC.OPACT`: canonical operatorization/linearization of that action;
3. future coupling construction;
4. `O-SPEC.STR`: physical transition-strength authority.

The first obligation should be tested before any further amplitude-like construction.
