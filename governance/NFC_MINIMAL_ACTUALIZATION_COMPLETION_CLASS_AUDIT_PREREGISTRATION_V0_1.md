## 3. Frozen question

> What is the weakest class of additional mathematical law that could convert NFC's source-forced admissibility/canonicalization structure into history-level physical actualization without circularity, hidden branch privilege, or mere relocation of the choice into auxiliary data?

The audit classifies completion classes only. It does not choose one, assign prior probability, claim physical truth, or modify NFC.

## 4. Qualifying requirements

Any qualifying completion class must satisfy the accepted shared selector core:

- C1 DOMAIN_INDEPENDENCE
- C2 NONTRIVIAL_SELECTIVITY
- C3 ENDOGENEITY relative to the proposed extended theory
- C4 NONCIRCULARITY
- C5 REPRESENTATION_INVARIANCE
- C6 SEMANTIC_PHYSICAL_FORCE
- C7 PROVENANCE_VISIBILITY
- C8 MULTIPLICITY_RESOLUTION

and the NFC-specific extensions:

- N1 HISTORY_LEVEL_DOMAIN
- N2 DYNAMIC_COMPATIBILITY
- N3 BRANCH_SOURCE_COMPATIBILITY for universal/source-level claims
- N4 STOCHASTIC_NORMALIZATION_IF_APPLICABLE
- N5 TEMPORAL_OR_GLOBAL_COHERENCE for global-history claims

Because frozen NFC is already accepted as source-nonforcing, any genuine qualifying completion must be explicitly recognized as an **extension** unless the audit discovers a contradiction requiring separate reconciliation.

## 5. Candidate completion classes to test

At minimum:

### K1 — Deterministic history selector

A law/function selecting one admissible history or equivalence class:

`S : H_adm -> H_actual`

or an equivalent unique-history rule.

### K2 — Stochastic actualization measure/kernel

A normalized source-compatible law assigning weights/probabilities over admissible histories:

`mu` on `H_adm`, or `K : state/history-prefix -> Prob(continuations)`.

### K3 — Variational/extremal selector

A source-compatible functional `A[h]`, order, cost, or extremal principle whose theorem force singles out one history/class or induces a normalized weighting law.

### K4 — Initial/boundary-condition completion

Declared initial or boundary data plus deterministic dynamics yielding one history.

The audit must decide whether this closes actualization or merely relocates it to the choice of initial/boundary data.

### K5 — Branch-local selector family

Each branch has its own selector/dynamics, possibly complete within branch scope.

The audit must test whether universal actualization then requires a branch/meta-selector and whether that creates regress or unresolved branch privilege.

### K6 — Recursive/fixed-point/self-consistency selector

A unique fixed history, attractor, consistency solution, or recursive closure rule.

It qualifies only if uniqueness/selection is over histories rather than representations/carriers and all hypotheses are supplied by the extended theory.

### K7 — Superselection / equivalence-class selector

A law that chooses a physical sector, branch, or equivalence class but not an individual history within it.

The audit must determine whether this is a full actualizer or only a first-stage selector requiring additional within-class selection.

### K8 — Constraint accumulation / maximal-consistency selector

Actuality is identified with the unique survivor of all admissibility/consistency constraints, if uniqueness can be proved.

The audit must distinguish true uniqueness from canonicalization or maximal admissibility.

### K9 — Meta-law over selector families

A higher-order principle selects among multiple admissible selector laws.

The audit must test regress and whether the meta-law itself is merely a renamed selector.

## 6. Nonqualifying shortcuts to test explicitly

- declaring the actual history as additional data;
- choosing an initial condition without a selection law;
- selecting a branch by fiat;
- using observed history/data retrospectively to fit a selector;
- calling MCS/UC/TSI uniqueness history uniqueness;
- defect minimization without a theorem giving physical actualization force;
- assuming equiprobability from absence of a selector;
- importing Born-rule-like, path-integral-like, least-action-like, anthropic, teleological, or stochastic rules without independent NFC derivation/provenance;
- adding a selector whose entire content is extensionally equivalent to listing the actual history.

## 7. Required analyses

1. **Minimal mathematical signature** for each K1–K9 class.
2. **C1–C8 / N1–N5 matrix** for each class.
3. **Relocation test** — identify where the selection burden moves if the class fails.
4. **Regress test** — determine whether a selector requires a selector of selectors.
5. **Branch-neutrality test** — identify whether universal claims silently privilege branch-specific structure.
6. **Representation test** — distinguish selecting a history from selecting a representative/canonical form.
7. **Extension-cost test** — identify the minimum genuinely new primitive content beyond frozen NFC.
8. **Hidden-solution reversal** — attempt to show one class is already realized by frozen NFC before classifying it as an extension.

## 8. Outcome taxonomy

A__ONE_MINIMAL_COMPLETION_CLASS_DOMINATES

One completion class is strictly weaker/more general than all alternatives while satisfying the requirements.

B__MULTIPLE_IRREDUCIBLE_COMPLETION_CLASSES

Two or more genuinely different minimal completion classes can satisfy the requirements; frozen NFC does not privilege among them.

C__ONLY_TWO_STAGE_OR_META_SELECTOR_COMPLETIONS_ARE_POSSIBLE

Any completion necessarily requires layered selection (e.g. branch then history), with no single-stage universal selector class available at current abstraction.

D__NO_NONCIRCULAR_COMPLETION_CLASS_IDENTIFIED

Every tested class fails endogeneity/noncircularity/physical-force requirements.

E__FROZEN_NFC_ALREADY_CONTAINS_A_QUALIFYING_COMPLETION

Contradicts accepted boundary; route to separate reconciliation.

F__UNDERDETERMINED_OR_REPAIR_REQUIRED

Source/protocol defects prevent responsible classification.

Exactly one primary outcome must be selected.

## 9. Important interpretation rule

A result that identifies multiple qualifying completion classes strengthens the accepted source-nonforcing conclusion: it shows not only that frozen NFC lacks a selector, but that more than one inequivalent completion can be added consistently at the level audited.

This does not establish that any completion is physically true.

## 10. Stop conditions

At execution end:

- create one Project Observatory audit artifact;
- select exactly one A–F outcome;
- do not mutate NFC, PGH, or FCP;
- do not propose a completion as accepted NFC physics;
- do not assign empirical support;
- route any contradiction to separate acceptance/reconciliation.
