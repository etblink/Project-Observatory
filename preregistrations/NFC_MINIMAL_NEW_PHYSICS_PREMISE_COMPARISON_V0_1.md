## Evaluation criteria

Each package is evaluated separately on:

1. **Added physical structure** — number and type of genuinely new physical commitments.
2. **NFC representation discipline** — compatibility with quotient visibility, no-smuggling, and source/branch scope.
3. **Mathematical closure** — whether the premise package defines a total coherent actualization law on its declared domain.
4. **Projective/sequential consistency** — whether finite-horizon statements cohere under extension/restriction.
5. **Tie/degeneracy burden** — whether unresolved multiplicity remains.
6. **Parameter burden** — whether arbitrary continuous/discrete parameters enter.
7. **Failure exposure** — whether the package has explicit mathematical and empirical ways to fail.
8. **Empirical discriminator potential** — whether a concrete model could in principle differ observationally from the rival under matched auxiliaries.
9. **Branch compatibility** — whether the package automatically contradicts any frozen branch theorem at declared scope.
10. **Auxiliary sensitivity** — dependence on horizon, hidden state, initial/boundary policy, or arbitrary ranking choices.

No scalar score is preregistered. Dominance requires criterion-by-criterion justification.

## Guardrails

- Simplicity is a modeling virtue, not evidence of physical truth.
- Parameter-free does not mean source-forced.
- Maximum entropy, Laplace indifference, Bayesian convention, quantum probability, or external stochastic principles may not be imported as evidence.
- Determinism may not receive a presumption merely because it uses point-valued outputs.
- Stochasticity may not receive a presumption merely because a normalized measure is mathematically natural.
- A deterministic package with undeclared tie-breaking fails.
- A stochastic package whose equal-weight premise is merely relabeled symmetry fails authority, though it may remain a valid explicit new postulate.
