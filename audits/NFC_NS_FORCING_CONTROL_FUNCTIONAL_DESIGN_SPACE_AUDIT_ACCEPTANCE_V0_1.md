## Review of NF-A

PASS.

The prospective scale-local force descriptor is a legitimate research class because it is defined from the forcing rather than the realized regularity outcome, does not vanish when `u_0=0`, and does not infer smallness from smooth compact support. The execution correctly refuses to select a norm/decomposition/threshold from frozen NFC where none is presently supplied.

NF-A is therefore a specification target, not a derived theorem.

## Review of NF-B

PASS.

The NFC-native transport-weighted source ledger is architecturally well motivated by the existing NS window/ledger/common-state machinery. The execution correctly preserves the typing distinction between external-source contribution and endogenous defect burden.

A future source-to-ledger bridge must be explicit; defining source events to be defect events by fiat would fail anti-smuggling.

## Work-injection review

PASS.

The audit correctly demotes cumulative work injection from first prospective descriptor to state-coupled continuation diagnostic unless a separate upstream bound expresses it in predeclared source data. This avoids circular use of the solution under investigation.

## OpenAI negative-control review

PASS.

The candidate classes are not tuned to the control. Neither treats zero initial data, bounded kinetic energy, smoothness, or compact support as sufficient for subcriticality. No coefficient or threshold was chosen from the known blowup outcome.

The control remains a future falsification benchmark, not a source of fitted parameters.

## Uniqueness review

Outcome A is not earned. NF-A and NF-B solve different problems: NF-A supplies physical/source-side quantitative description; NF-B supplies NFC-native bookkeeping/transport integration. Neither dominates the other at present.
