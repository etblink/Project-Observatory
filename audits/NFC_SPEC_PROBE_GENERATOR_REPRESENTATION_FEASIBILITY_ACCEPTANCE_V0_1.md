## Book-I process review

PASS.

Book I's admissible processes are typed maps into finite expanded relational structures, not generally endomorphisms of one fixed state space. Closure under typed composition therefore does not imply a representation into `End(H)` or any other fixed operator algebra.

This is a structural reason, not merely an omitted theorem, that generic processhood is weaker than operatorhood.

## SPEC probe-definition review

PASS.

A SPEC probe is a declared admissible process whose effect on the certified observable class is branch-visible and accountable. That definition does not contain a unique linear/operator realization. Probe episodes and transition ledgers supply quotient-visible effects and composition data, which are already captured by the accepted relational/ledger representation.

## Generator-compatibility review

PASS.

The theorem that probes exercise spectral modes of `L_SPEC` and induce transition-accessible spectral packets is an accessibility/compatibility theorem. It does not define a map `P -> V_P` or identify each probe with one spectral mode or generator.

The execution correctly blocks that promotion.

## Restricted-subclass review

PASS.

The corpus certainly contains objects already declared as operators/generators, and a future probe may be defined directly in terms of such an object. But the frozen SPEC probe class does not currently contain a source-selected nontrivial subclass equipped with a theorem assigning a unique operator realization to every member.

Therefore Outcome B is not earned.

This does not forbid later identification of such a subclass.

## RH review

PASS.

RH is a constructive precedent, not a transfer theorem. Its operator-origin route succeeds only after the branch supplies both a transformation family and a branch-specific aggregation/weighting law. The accepted audit correctly identifies those as the kind of extra representation data generic SPEC lacks.

## LING review

PASS.

LING demonstrates that lawful quotient-visible action and grammar/compositional closure can exist without linear operator representation. This supports, rather than weakens, the conclusion that process-level action is a coherent stopping point.

## YM/SM landing review

PASS.

YM and SM provide rich target operator arenas, but target-space existence is not a representation map. The missing object remains a lawful assignment of a generic SPEC probe to an element/operator in those arenas.

## Nonforcing construction review

PASS.

A fixed relation/support can be represented by multiple inequivalent numerical operators after additional choices, while the same source-forced semantics can also remain at the Boolean/relational level. This suffices to establish that the frozen corpus does not force a unique generic operator representation.

The acceptance does not claim that every such numerical realization is physically lawful; only that the source-forced relation does not uniquely determine one.

## Outcome-E rejection review

PASS.

It would be too strong to claim no route exists. RH supplies an existence proof of a general architectural strategy for operator reconstruction, and SPEC/YM/SM supply plausible target arenas. The proper result is nonforcing at current scope, not impossibility.
