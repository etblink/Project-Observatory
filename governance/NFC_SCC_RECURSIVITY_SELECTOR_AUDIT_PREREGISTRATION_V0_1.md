## 4. Frozen primary question

Does the frozen canonical SCC branch, using its recursive stabilization, CapRec, path/cap equivalence, MCS, UC, TSI, persistence-selection, transported-invariant, and closure machinery, force a nontrivial actual-history selector over admissible histories?

More precisely:

> Does SCC recursive stabilization plus its canonical carrier/completion machinery collapse physical-history multiplicity to one selected history, one source-forced equivalence class with physical actualization force, or a source-forced probability/weighting law over histories; or does SCC only constrain, classify, distinguish, identify, or canonically represent histories while leaving multiple admissible histories physically unselected?

## 5. Selector criterion

A qualifying SCC selector must do more than any of the following:

- remove non-admissible histories;
- define persistence-selected objects;
- distinguish histories structurally;
- establish path-equivalence or cap-equivalence;
- establish confluence;
- produce a minimal faithful carrier;
- establish uniqueness of a carrier representation;
- construct a branchwise completion or colimit;
- establish endpoint identification;
- prove a fixed point, attractor, stabilization, or canonical normal form unless that result has actual-history selection force.

A qualifying selector must provide at least one of:

1. deterministic actual-history selection from admissible alternatives;
2. a source- or SCC-forced probability/measure/kernel/weighting law over alternatives;
3. a variational/extremal principle whose theorem force identifies the physically realized history or history class;
4. a unique-history theorem whose hypotheses are themselves forced at the audited scope;
5. an equivalent explicit actualization mechanism.

A theorem selecting a unique *representation* of a history or carrier does not qualify unless uniqueness of representation is proved to imply uniqueness of physically realized history.

## 6. Central ambiguity to test

The audit must explicitly distinguish:

HISTORY_DISCRIMINATION = the ability of SCC data/invariants to tell distinct histories apart.

HISTORY_CLASSIFICATION = the partition of histories into path-equivalent, cap-confluent, or genuinely distinct classes.

CARRIER_CANONICALITY = existence/uniqueness of minimal faithful carrier or completion object.

HISTORY_ACTUALIZATION = a law identifying which admissible history/history class is physically realized.

No inference from the first three to the fourth is permitted without an explicit theorem or unavoidable derivation.

## 11. Outcome taxonomy

A__SCC_ACTUAL_HISTORY_SELECTOR_SOURCE_FORCED

SCC establishes a genuine actual-history selector and all hypotheses needed for it are forced strongly enough by the common NFC source to challenge the prior source-nonforcing finding.

B__SCC_ACTUAL_HISTORY_SELECTOR_BRANCH_SPECIFIC_OR_CONDITIONAL

SCC establishes a genuine actual-history selector, but it depends on branch-specific declarations or conditional hypotheses not forced by the common NFC source.

C__SCC_RECURSIVITY_CANONICALIZES_WITHOUT_ACTUALIZING

SCC recursion/closure establishes stability, classification, confluence, unique carrier representation, completion, or endpoint canonicality, but does not choose which admissible history is physically actual. Multiple admissible histories remain possible at the audited scope.

D__SCC_PROVIDES_PARTIAL_SELECTION_PRESSURE_ONLY

SCC narrows or ranks histories beyond bare admissibility but does not establish a complete actual-history selector. Any pressure/ranking must be explicitly identified and its force bounded.

E__UNDERDETERMINED__SCC_SCOPE_OR_DEPENDENCY_INSUFFICIENT

The frozen corpus does not permit a sound adjudication between selector and non-selector interpretations.

F__REPAIR_REQUIRED_BEFORE_ADJUDICATION

A formal inconsistency, broken dependency, scope defect, or authority problem prevents responsible adjudication.

No outcome counts as success merely because it preserves or damages NFC.

## 12. Acceptance thresholds

Outcome A or B requires:

- exact theorem/source identification;
- noncircular selector force;
- distinction from carrier/representation uniqueness;
- explicit handling of multiple-history cases;
- scope match;
- hypothesis provenance;
- resistance to a conservative-extension or alternative-history countermodel.

Outcome C requires positive evidence that SCC closure can coexist with more than one admissible history/history representative or that its uniqueness theorems are confined to carrier/equivalence/representation scope rather than actualization.

Outcome D requires an explicit theorem-level narrowing/ranking mechanism stronger than admissibility but weaker than actualization.

Search failure alone cannot justify C or D.
