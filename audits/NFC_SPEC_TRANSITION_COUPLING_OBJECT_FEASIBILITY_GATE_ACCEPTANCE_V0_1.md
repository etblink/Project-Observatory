## Key review findings

PASS.

The execution correctly identifies the strongest latent ingredients without overpromoting them:

- SPEC has a certified operator packet and transition-accessible spectral packets;
- SPEC has admissible probes and quotient-visible response signatures;
- YM supplies an invariant bilinear form on certified root/operator directions;
- SM/SPEC supplies a matter-extended operator arena.

But the frozen corpus does not provide a theorem-level map placing the admissible probe into the same interaction space as parent/child spectral modes, nor a theorem promoting any resulting overlap/coupling quantity to physical transition strength.

The result therefore cannot be strengthened to Outcome A or B.

## Bilinear-form review

PASS.

The YM bilinear form is a genuine structural object and could plausibly participate in a future coupling construction. However, its existence alone is insufficient because the probe lacks a frozen representation in that space and because no frozen theorem identifies a bilinear magnitude with a transition rate.

This is exactly the distinction the gate was designed to protect.

## Matter-extension review

PASS.

The matter-extended operator enlarges the available operator space but does not itself provide a probe-dependent interaction term with channel-specific amplitudes. Treating the mere existence of a larger Hermitian operator as a transition-strength law would be an unjustified upgrade.

## Conservative-extension review

PASS.

Different lawful probe-interaction/coupling extensions can preserve all audited frozen SPEC/YM/SM theorem truths while producing different branching fractions. That establishes nonforcing at current scope.

## Minimal missing burdens

The decomposition into two burdens is accepted:

1. `O-SPEC.INT-REP` — a source/branch-visible probe-interaction representation;
2. `O-SPEC.STR` — a theorem promoting the resulting coupling quantity to physical transition-strength force.

Neither burden subsumes the other.

Accordingly Outcome D remains stronger and better justified than Outcome E.
