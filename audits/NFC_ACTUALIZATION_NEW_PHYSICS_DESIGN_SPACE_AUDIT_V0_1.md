## Why the other classes do not remain irreducible completion classes

### K3 — variational/extremal

A variational law becomes an actualizer only if its extremal rule resolves the lawful multiplicity. If it produces one actual continuation, it instantiates K1. If it assigns physical weights over multiple extrema, it instantiates K2. If several extrema remain without weights, the actualization gap survives.

K3 therefore remains a potentially useful **generator mechanism**, but not a third irreducible endpoint class.

### K4 — attractor/fixed point

A unique physically enforced attractor gives a deterministic continuation class and therefore instantiates K1. Basin weights or transition probabilities instantiate K2. Multiple unweighted attractors merely relocate the selection problem.

K4 is an implementation family, not an additional irreducible completion class.

### K5 — symmetry breaking / boundary / initial condition

Merely specifying boundary or initial data does not explain why those data are actual. A law uniquely selecting the data instantiates K1; a law weighting them instantiates K2. Superselection or spontaneous-symmetry-breaking sectors likewise require either unique outcome force or physical weights to close actualization.

K5 can supply physically important structure, but by itself it does not escape K1/K2.

### K6 — observation-coupled actualization

K6 is the **best NFC-native coupling locus**, because frozen NFC already contains lawful internal testing, quotient-visible observation, persistent records, and certified probe-induced transitions. However, an observation-coupled law must still say what happens among lawful post-observation continuations. A unique continuation is K1; nontrivial physical weights are K2.

Thus K6 is not a third completion class. It is a particularly well-motivated place to instantiate either K1 or K2.

### K7 — empirically anchored phenomenological law

K7 is scientifically legitimate only when an empirical regularity requires it. It can be model-level physics even if no fundamental derivation exists. But no current NFC evidence in the frozen corpus supplies such an empirical regularity for actualization.

K7 therefore remains a future empirical route, not a present fundamental completion-class winner.

### K8 — global/self-consistency law

A global consistency condition can reduce the lawful history set. If exactly one history survives, it instantiates K1. If multiple histories survive with physical weights, it instantiates K2. If several survive unweighted, the actualization gap remains.

K8 is therefore a constraint/generator family rather than a third irreducible endpoint class.

### K9 — hybrid

Hybridization may be required by a future concrete theory, but it increases assumption and parameter burden. No hybrid presently dominates a simpler K1 or K2 representative.

### K10 — no justified class

The no-preference reversal succeeds against any **unique** class choice: there is presently no justification for choosing K1 over K2. But K10 is too strong as the primary result because K1 and K2 both satisfy the preregistered feasibility standard for **candidate construction**: each can in principle close the actualization type gap, preserve NFC scope discipline, remain failure-exposed, and be instantiated under matched auxiliaries.

Therefore candidate construction is justified as a comparative test program even though class adoption is not justified.

## Book-I compatibility

Book I explicitly does not take probability space as primitive and requires later structures to earn their license. This blocks declaring K2 primitive without a bridge, but it does not prohibit a later lawfully introduced probability structure. The same licensing discipline applies to K1: a deterministic actualizer cannot be smuggled in as hidden identity or raw-label recovery.

Therefore both K1 and K2 face real but symmetric provenance burdens.

## Book-VII compatibility

Book VII's status/scope/promotion machinery can house either K1 or K2 as a conditional or bridge structure provided its dependencies and regime are explicit. Neither class gains source-level force merely by being declared.

This preserves the prior finding that governance can certify a future actualization law but cannot choose which physics is correct.

## Minimal-extension reversal

The strongest minimal-extension route is not a unique class but a **matched pair** localized at the most NFC-native interface:

- `D_obs`: observation-coupled deterministic candidate, producing a unique lawful post-observation continuation.
- `S_obs`: observation-coupled genuinely stochastic candidate, producing a normalized nontrivial measure over the same lawful continuation domain.

Both must use identical:

- post-observation state domain;
- observable map;
- hidden-state policy;
- initial/boundary policy;
- branch/scope declaration;
- parameter-count policy where possible;
- intervention policy;
- decision functional.

Only the actualization rule may differ unless a difference is explicitly unavoidable and charged as an auxiliary asymmetry.

This matched-pair architecture is preferred as a **research design**, not as evidence that observation is physically the actualizer.

## Empirical ceiling

No class-level empirical discrimination is possible yet. Concrete candidate laws must be specified first. A future candidate pair must expose at least one observable regime in which, under matched auxiliaries,

`O(D_obs, A) != O(S_obs, A)`.

If no such regime can be produced, the pair is empirically non-identifiable and should not advance to experiment.

## Failure exposure

This design-space result would be downgraded if any of the following were established:

1. a third irreducible actualization class that does not reduce to deterministic selection or a nontrivial physical measure;
2. a frozen-source theorem that privileges K1 or K2;
3. a branch theorem whose certified content is contradicted by every admissible K1 or every admissible K2 completion;
4. an empirical regularity already bound to NFC that forces K7 or otherwise breaks the K1/K2 parity.

No such result was identified in this audit.
