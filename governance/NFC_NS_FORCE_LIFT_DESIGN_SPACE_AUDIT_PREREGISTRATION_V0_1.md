## Question

Given the accepted obstruction `T_NS-FORCE-LIFT`, what is the smallest noncircular design class capable of lifting a declared smooth compactly supported continuum forcing field into a lawful NFC `EXOGENOUS_SOURCE_FAMILY` without simply reversing Book VI by fiat?

## Frozen candidate classes

- L1 — arbitrary basis/test-family encoding of the continuum force into source coefficients.
- L2 — finite relational approximant / sampling-refinement system with a separately proved reconstruction or convergence theorem.
- L3 — categorical adjunction/right-lift style construction giving a universal continuum-to-source lift.
- L4 — branch-specific discretization/encoding functor with explicit support, additivity, scaling, quotient-visibility and refinement-consistency obligations.
- L5 — dual-observable/pairing lift using the action of the force on a declared complete separating family of test observables.
- L6 — direct declaration `F -> tag(F)` or equivalent relabeling.
- L7 — no qualified construction class at current scope.

## Mandatory gates

A surviving lift must:

1. preserve zero, addition and scalar rescaling on the declared force class;
2. preserve declared support/time-support provenance to the extent required for later localization;
3. be invariant under irrelevant presentation choices;
4. be prospectively specified before using outcome data;
5. possess a reconstruction/separation property strong enough that distinct physically relevant forces are not silently identified;
6. avoid dependence on a basis, mesh, partition or sampling rule unless its equivalence/canonicity is separately proved;
7. not identify continuum scale with NFC window identity by declaration;
8. not smuggle endpoint forcing back into Book-I primitives;
9. preserve the distinction between source representation, later source localization, later source transport and endogenous defect;
10. remain compatible with the OpenAI smooth compactly supported forced-blowup construction as a negative control rather than excluding it by class definition.

## Adversarial tests

- reverse-interface fallacy;
- basis dependence;
- discretization aliasing;
- failure of reconstruction;
- support/provenance loss;
- quotient/presentation dependence;
- arbitrary target/source cardinality matching;
- hidden continuum ontology at Book-I level;
- empirical retrofit.

## Outcome taxonomy

- A — canonical lift already derivable from frozen NFC.
- B — one or more noncircular candidate lift schemas survive, with explicit new bridge burden.
- C — mathematically plausible lift classes exist but all require unresolved noncanonical choices beyond one explicit bridge premise.
- D — no nonarbitrary lift class survives at audited scope.
- E — source side too weak to represent declared forcing class without changing foundational identity.
- F — underdetermined.
- G — repair required.
